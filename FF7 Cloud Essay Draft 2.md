# The Assassination of Cloud Strife: How the *FFVII* Remakes Murdered a Hero and Gave Us a Psychopath

Cloud Strife was never meant to be a loud display of psychosis. In 1997, his narrative was a compelling identity crisis grounded in realistic biology. His character was that of a hero struggling with **traumatic brain injury**, not that of a mental patient being exploited for shock value. The original game shows a desperate act for survival, his mind fabricating a fiction from the legacy of a fallen comrade, and his refusal to surrender to an alien parasite within him. And when that fiction failed, he was restored by his childhood ally, who reminded him that his body was fundamentally his own. Ultimately, the story of him and his friends is a profound testament to human will. With his team's fierce compassion and loyalty, the unbreakable resolve of Cloud Strife conquered his affliction, and his formidable leadership saved the entire world.

Where the original game champions human vulnerability, the *Remake* trilogy whimpers a cowardly defeat. It subverts a monumental achievement of human agency by degrading it into a loud, theatrical meltdown. It formulaically replaces profound human truths with hollow imitations completely unfit for a human audience. Consequently, by transforming Cloud's internal crisis into a series of violent outbursts, the new games turn him into a public liability and catastrophically damage the entire cast. What was once a highly competent group of rebels has now been reduced to little more than a network of negligent enablers forced to babysit a psychopath.

## From Silent Trauma to Cinematic Seizure

To understand this narrative collapse, we must look at how the remakes fundamentally alter the presentation of Cloud's trauma. In 1997, his lapses in thought were quiet and internal, presented via text boxes and high-pitched cues. This allowed him to remain composed in front of his allies, while players drew secret insights from his thoughts. Far from appearing as a volatile risk, he appeared as a charismatic, efficient, and reliable leader. The worst you could have accused the man of was that he sometimes got a migraine.

But more than that, he possessed a strong gift for oration, by delivering a complex, hours-long history lesson in Kalm with perfect composure before his crew. His voice was perfectly attuned to his somber story, captivating the social atmosphere of the group. Throughout his presentation, the party themselves asked clarifying questions with deep empathy and respect, to which he responded with forthright answers. They reacted with sorrow and awe as he masterfully described Sephiroth's slip and descent into madness. The group treated him with immense dignity, hanging onto his every word, and imbibing the depth of the threat he was leading them to chase down.

And then, just as he was about to detail the climactic battle between him and Sephiroth, in a singular instant his memory blanked. He confided simply that it was the end of his story. When Barret, enthralled with his masterful oration up to that point, grew upset about the anticlimax, Cloud simply shrugged. As the group tried to reconstruct what must have happened, he admitted that he too would like to know how he survived. Because he was entirely responsive and possessed a complete presence of mind, it is easy to see how the party attributed his memory loss to the trauma of the events, or perhaps to being bested by Sephiroth. What neither they nor Cloud himself yet knew was that the truth ran much deeper.

While it is important to acknowledge the effects of Jenova cells and how they crush the will of their host, Cloud's amnesia was entirely a product of **Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI)** caused by extreme Mako-poisoning. This is a process that normally destroys a person's memory entirely. He survived this terminal threat not once but twice, through the intervention of two distinct lifelines: Zack Fair, whose constant presence and care sustained him through a prolonged catatonic state, and Tifa Lockhart, who later brought him back from the brink of annihilation, using their combined memories of a shared childhood to anchor him to his past.

His adoption of the 1st class SOLDIER persona was a matter of **Confabulation**, a biological symptom of severe TBI where the mind automatically fills blanks using whatever external stimuli may be at hand. On the way home to Midgar, Zack prattled about his plans for them to become mercenaries, and as he did he unintentionally afforded Cloud a blueprint which his mind used to absorb every last detail. Even the squats Zack so vigorously performed in the back of the truck were not missed. And after the tragic death of his friend, Cloud awoke dressed in a uniform not his own, a nearby Buster Sword at hand. Far from some ego-driven facade to hide behind, his mind simply saw these as evident facts of life. And later at the sector 7 train station, when he recognized the face of his childhood crush, his fragmented mind was electrified into a coherent whole. Ex-SOLDIER, 1st class, mercenary for hire.

Throughout the story, his condition was complicated by struggles not with random bouts of hallucination, but rather biological overrides through the parasitical Jenova cells whenever his greatest foe Sephiroth was in close proximity. This was explicitly visualized when the player saw a manifestation of Cloud's inner child, desperately trying and failing to stop his adult body from handing over the Black Materia to Sephiroth at the Temple of the Ancients. It was further proven at the Forgotten City altar, where Cloud's core consciousness actively fought against an external command to strike his faithful ally Aerith with his sword. And finally, it was concretely cemented when Cloud fully succumbed to the Jenova 'Reunion' at the Northern Crater. The narrative took the most drastic and sensible action possible by removing him from the party entirely, forcing Cid Highwind to assume the mantle of captain while Tifa Lockhart became Cloud's dutiful caretaker.

It is from this summit of human resilience and tragic vulnerability that the modern remakes graciously invite us to run headfirst off a cliff, plunging us straight into the depths of a meta-sequel trilogy. Here, the unyielding 'genius' of the writers takes the audience on a rollercoaster of chronic mental illness, covering for an absolute bankruptcy of narrative depth. Cloud constantly drops in and out of sanity while a hardened resistance faction blindly cedes military command to him, at no point acknowledging the catastrophic cost to their own cause. The original's silent trauma is entirely replaced by loud cinematic spectacle, complete with a writer's pen bursting through the script whenever possible, and a villainous metaphorical representation of the player-base's grievances debased to nothing more than annoying whispers on the wind.

## From Companions to Appliances

To understand how completely the modern trilogy throws out the credibility of its cast, look at what it does to its female leads. In the 1997 original, Tifa Lockhart and Aerith Gainsborough are not brainless cheerleaders who mindlessly validate a psychotic protagonist. They are authentic, mature allies joined with Cloud through their shared trauma, navigated by rationality. The writers kidnap these characters, bind them, take them out back, and hack away at their parts until they can squish them into the Honey Bee Inn with Cloud. Consider the sheer hypocrisy of anyone cheering for a brain-damaged veteran forced into a cabaret for their amusement, only to backpedal when the writers exact their pen on Tifa and Aerith. First they exclaim a 'progressive triumph of self-expression', now they cry foul play and allege a series of offenses? They are utterly blind to the ink-stains they have on their own hands, for every single one of these characters has been brutally assaulted, disfigured, and hollowed out. Whether it's by forcing Cloud to execute sexy stage routines or by reducing Tifa and Aerith into competing corporate sex-bots, the result is identical. It trades three of the greatest characters of all time for a glorified dollhouse, ensuring that the very comrades who propelled Cloud to excellence are now just lifeless mannequins stitched together from butchered parts.

### The Gaslit Survivor
Tifa’s position in the original story explored what authentic trauma actually looks like when you have a leader with a realistically compromised mind. Her behavior made perfect sense when one understands that Cloud suffered from a severe traumatic brain injury further impaired by confabulation. It allowed him to unintentionally and flawlessly lie without actually being a liar. He genuinely believed his own mind's fabrications, and displayed none of the behavioral tells, eye shifts, or hesitations of a deceiver. Instead, his delivery of history was filled with absolute conviction, likely with an unwitting, prophetic stare establishing a false authority over the past.

When we consider that Tifa suffered her own near-fatal blackout during the events Cloud related in Kalm, she was left completely defenseless and caught in a psychological nightmare where she could not trust her own memories. She was compelled to remain silent by Cloud's natural talents for leadership malfunctioning as weapons of utterly convincing deceit. Her initial reservation was not a strategic mind game or an attempt to coddle a fragile friend. It was the direct fallout of a flawlessly constructed delusion, a confabulation so detailed and precise, and one taking on the guise of a firsthand witness account, that it effectively gaslit Tifa into doubting her own eyes and memories. Perhaps she was the one remembering the past incorrectly, and that terrifying prospect meant risking the entire group branding her as crazy.

This fear led her to embrace her vulnerability with a bravery so great that the act of following Cloud into the poisonous Lifestream pales in comparison. For she finally confessed the truth, and that very act was what saved him. Laying everything on the table, she admitted that she was too afraid to tell anyone that she remembered the past differently because she 'didn't know what would happen' and 'didn't know what to do.' Tifa finally confronted a biologically rewired brain operating with absolute, terrifying conviction and said no. By facing her own horror, she shared with him the bravery required to face the truth of who he was, which finally brought Cloud back from the dead. This beautiful picture completely vindicates these two epic friends, allies, and even lovers of any wrongdoing. They proved how a friendship forged through fierce loyalty, trust, and battle-scarred hearts could defeat even the most intimate of enemies: themselves.

The writers of the remake trilogy completely betray this psychological honesty by infecting the script with their own severe case of imposter syndrome. In a desperate bid to make the characters appear half-human, the new timeline features a Cloud who prematurely questions his own identity, even as early as the events after the insanely derailed Gongaga Reactor fiasco. By having Cloud confess his doubts to Tifa, the script accidentally portrays her as an incomprehensible coward, because she now knows he shares her own suspicions, but she still chooses to actively withhold a broken man's identity from him. One way or another, her silence is a product of sheer terror, making her either a battered housewife walking on eggshells to save her life, or an irresponsible enabler coddling a violent madman under the guise of safety. Unless one assumes that she is under threat of the writers siccing more whispers on him, she is far from protecting Cloud and championing his health. She has become the gaslighter herself, and is now nothing more than a prop to be used at the writers' earliest convenience. But at least, considering this is how they ruined the slow-burn identity crisis and the grand revelation that made the original narrative so powerful, it's not like they could commit a more egregious crime. They couldn't possibly execute a worse hit on Tifa using Cloud.

### The Ghost in the Gestures
If Tifa's tragedy began with silence, then Aerith’s was unraveled by Zack's echo. Her relationship with Cloud represented the profound heartbreak of falling in love with a ghost. In the original portrayal, her attraction to him was far from some pre-destined fairy tale carried on the whispers of a stale wind. It was a deeply human, bittersweet accident born entirely from the nostalgia of a long lost love. She was initially drawn in by the rewired, post-traumatic confabulations, which relied heavily on the physical mannerisms, speech, and bravado of Zack Fair. And in turn, Cloud's imperfect imitation provided him the serendipitous position of both not knowing who Aerith was and yet simultaneously feeling comfortably familiar with her, almost as if she was a lost memory caught on the tip of his tongue. But this adoption of Zack's persona reflected to Aerith a man whom she knew to be dead. Without yet knowing it, she was glimpsing the faded but still living blueprint of a man brought back from the grave.

Their first meeting was a happy coincidence. During Cloud's daring escape, he came across this comely girl who offered to sell him a flower for a paltry sum. The encounter was short and sweet, enough to catch Aerith's attention, but not enough to give either any suspicion that there was something special about the other. This restrained expert writing directly set up his later dramatic entrance into Aerith's Sector 5 church. A handsome stranger came crashing through the roof, safeguarded by the very flowers she so diligently grew. Just as this sudden arrival stole her attention, so too did his remarkable demeanor steal her trusting heart. Projecting Zack's echo everywhere he went, his presence was simply too much to ignore. And as she openly made plain at the Sector 6 playground, it was his physical composition, the way he walked, stood, and carried himself, that had enthralled her senses, reminding her of her deceased boyfriend. Cloud’s confabulation inadvertently created a flawlessly camouflaged net.

Yet, as we see later in the story, Aerith's sharp emotional intelligence allowed her to see right through the holes of this mesh. During their iconic date at the Gold Saucer, she explicitly told Cloud that she wanted to meet his true self. She clearly saw something past Zack's stolen persona. Cloud responded with confusion, that he didn't quite know what she meant and that he was right in front of her. We could see the tragedy of a man who truly doesn't know who he is, but it elevated Aerith to nothing less than a profoundly elegant and charming young woman, capable of poking at the very depths of delusion itself. While Tifa was too afraid to dare, Aerith did it with such grace that it was only when it was too late that Cloud would ever realize the meaning behind her words, or that Tifa would finally learn from her example.

Completely throwing nuance to the wind, the remake trilogy obliterates the brilliant balance of these characters by thoroughly bricking the entire process from the start. Their first meeting is bloated into a supernatural event where Sephiroth manifests as a hallucination. Then literal farts from the writer's pen physically swarm the characters for instant trailer bait. Aerith is granted meta-aware knowledge via Ancient magic, and treats Cloud from their very first hours as if with a wink toward the camera. With mechanical precision, she behaves entirely as if awaiting her next line, that is, whenever the writers should bother to excrete it. And worst of all, rather than becoming enamored by the echoes of Zack and Cloud's natural charm, she cryptically confesses her love for secrets by playing oracle, actively concealing future truths from her own allies. She is reduced to a corporate script made flesh, turning what was once a masterpiece of human complexity into nothing more than a hollow, minimum-viable epitaph of her former self.

## From Acute Vulnerability to Chronic Liability

Modern interpretations of a nearly thirty-year-old game would have us believe that Cloud Strife was merely the product of childhood insecurities utilizing a false persona to defend a fragile ego. A weakling who could not face the truth of the world until his friends charitably put him back together again. The severe Mako-poisoning and the alien parasite that Dr. Hojo forced in his body? Simply complications playing secondary roles. By all rights Cloud Strife should really have been wholly inferior to the mindless black-robed 'Sephiroth Clones' roaming the world of Gaia. For surely his mental illness was so profoundly disadvantageous to him that he would have died immediately, had it not been for the stroke of the writers' pen allowing him a mind of his own.

As we have already explored, this is complete rubbish. It is a retroactive fiction, born from an insatiable desire to project the narrative failures of later spin-offs and remakes onto a story that utterly outclasses them. Much like the Jenova cells that infested Cloud's body, these modern revisions attempt to supplant and overtake the legacy of a genuine powerhouse of gaming, a character that led his team to total victory against insurmountable odds. Far from being a failed experiment, Cloud actually extracted himself from Hojo's equations through sheer strength of will and the ferocious loyalty of his allies. That is why his darkest moment in the original game remains a monumental hallmark of gaming history. It was the crisis of a heroic leader, not the capitulation of a fragile coward.

### Acute Vulnerability
Prior to the Temple of the Ancients and in the eyes of his companions, Cloud operated with a flawless track record of tactical execution and reliable leadership. It's no accident that he was voted commander after leaving Midgar, because he possessed the necessary confidence and military competence to serve as the group's sole compass. Even as Barret later admitted during Cloud's sickness after the events of the Northern Crater, it was his identification, prioritization, and complete understanding of the true threat to the planet that uniquely qualified him as leader.

So when the party witnessed Cloud completely buckle and surrender the Black Materia to their mortal enemy, just to then physically assault Aerith immediately afterwards, they correctly recognized this calamity constituted nothing less than a catastrophic medical emergency. His violence had so quickly surged after Sephiroth's psychic paralysis lifted, that no matter the teammate the player had chosen for their party, they reacted with a singular, unified voice. They all screamed for Cloud to stop his vicious attack, and when words failed them, they paid violence for violence, physically battering their own leader until he was limp and unconscious. This sudden whiplash was so jarring that the trauma grabbed ahold of the player themselves. It was completely unexpected. At no point did the story suggest that such a betrayal was possible, that Cloud would so viciously attack a favored ally, a woman he had personally taken pains to save and to protect, with such an outburst of madness and unyielding brutality.

At this stage, while the team knew of Jenova, they did not yet understand the horror of the alien parasite vying for Cloud's soul. Nonetheless, they correctly discerned the event as a mental hijack triggered by Sephiroth's close proximity. When Cloud's biological override made him comply with the enemy's commands, his teammates all saw the strings being pulled right before their eyes, and how he had become a passenger in his own body. Importantly, Aerith herself understood the situation perfectly. She knew the stakes and the war they were fighting, and chose to separate from the party to play out her uniquely necessary role to save the world, not because she was afraid of Cloud but afraid *for* him. She would rather face the danger alone than see Cloud's body savagely abused and used against his will again. She understood that *physical* distance from Sephiroth was the only solution, and she knew that the enemy would follow her, for she was the true target on the cosmic chessboard.

And Cloud knew this, for while unconscious, he dreamed of Aerith running off on her own. She implored him not to worry, to not let this tragedy consume him. But even so, upon waking and knowing full well what he had done to her, the nightmare did not end. In a moment of tender vulnerability toward Tifa and Barret, he openly admitted his fear of what Sephiroth could make him do. Weathered and without his usual grit, he confessed his doubts about continuing his command, submerged in a profound sense of susceptibility, yet tempered by raw honesty. Rather than allowing their leader to falter, they diligently pursued his recovery. Without missing a beat, Barret confronted Cloud's failing with full force. Yes, he was responsible, but the worst thing he could do was give up now, especially when Aerith needed them most. Tifa intimately reminded Cloud with the faith of an acolyte that she believed in him, ever his devoted friend and follower. After setting the record straight, and with a heavy dose of Barret's tough love reviving his agency, Cloud realized that they needed him to carry this weight. By leveraging Tifa's unwavering loyalty and Barret's uncharacteristic gentleness, he knew he could once again shoulder the burden of leadership. And with a stalwart pledge that if he should lose control again, they would be there to snap him out of it, this compassionate yet tactical team intervention proved that even in his lowest moments, Cloud's innate sense of duty fiercely prioritized the safety of his comrades above his own, and that they valued him more than he could ever value himself.

This authentic affirmation is what gave Cloud his strength during the earth-shattering events at the Forgotten City altar. When he drew his sword at Sephiroth's command to slay Aerith, he trembled violently in extreme resistance. Yet, his will would have eventually failed him had it not been for the power of his team's promise, bringing him back from the brink with their collective voice. Much to his credit, Cloud heard them, and the strings of the puppet master were cut. Instead of giving up on their leader, it made complete sense to them that they must help a compromised friend fight off an active assault, just as they must fight off an evil predator trying to devour their planet. This hard-earned loyalty revealed their deep compassion for a man who, even when subjected to total psychic domination, struggled mightily in defiance as the world crumbled around him. Far from viewing him as a liability, the party understood the terrifying scale of their enemy's power and correctly recognized Cloud as their greatest asset. From their perspective, they knew that if any one of them had been in the same position, they would have turned their weapon upon Aerith without so much as a flinch. They knew that if anyone had the sheer strength of will to withstand the howling malice of Sephiroth’s magic, it would have to be Cloud. This unwavering bond was cemented when he resolutely declared his intent to hunt Sephiroth down. He requested them to follow him because they had proven to him that their strength lay in their unity. He plainly admitted he needed them, and this absolute clarity of heart, mind, and will inspired them to move forward, proving that even in their darkest hour, he remained a beacon of absolute resolve who would lead them to the literal ends of the earth.

This trajectory of internal resilience reached its absolute, unassailable climax in the game’s final battle, *not* when Tifa followed Cloud into the Lifestream during the events of Mideel. It's true that when she initiated a mind-link via the medium of Mako, they were allowed to collectively piece their tragic backstory together and cure Cloud of his brain injury, releasing him from the chains of confabulation. But it did nothing to thwart his continual tormentor, the parasitic Jenova cells preying within his body. It was only after the party executed the one-winged angel that Cloud faced that final remaining threat, an assault that Tifa, who had been steadfast by Cloud throughout everything, could only helplessly stand nearby to watch. Cloud entered the depths of his own mind and located the parasite, in the form of a human Sephiroth leering as he awaited his arrival. With complete composure, he annihilated the pestilence that had so viciously plagued him by using his ultimate attack: his unshakable will.

Thus did Cloud Strife regain his sovereignty by exhibiting one final act of complete self-mastery. His mind was put back together, his body was purged of the parasite, and he was once again a whole person. Because of the efforts of his team actively intervening whenever Sephiroth tried to claim his soul, Cloud was fortified when he did stumble. And stumble he did, whenever the threat came in close proximity, which they all did know and see. Rather than letting this acute, albeit extreme vulnerability break him, he weaponized the faith of his friends and the strength he found within him, a strength that had seen him through every challenge that would kill lesser men. In the end, his vulnerability was a temporary crisis, and not the defining trait of his character. No, that defining trait was solely reserved for his heroism, the bravery that his beloved team witnessed and testified to. They convinced him to use it, and they combined their efforts to propel him to the apex of greatness, so that the sacrifices of friends long past would not be spent in vain, and so that they all might be spared the fate of an alien parasite trying to take over every living thing they ever loved. Each and every one of them believed in him, and so it was that they all saved the world together as a team, through the undefeated leadership of Cloud Strife.

### Chronic Liability
Within roughly thirty to forty-five minutes, depending on how much rope you give it, the remake trilogy immediately hangs itself by introducing Cloud's psychological decay as the daily baseline. This debut comes complete with an out-of-body hallucination of Sephiroth and a burning Nibelheim. Rather than remaining in his head, his crumbling mind projects this walking plot device into his physical reality, only for him to vanish again just as quickly, much like the narrative's logic. But he waltzes in once again and Cloud stumbles his way into a back alley after him, escorted by the long silver locks of his in-and-out delusion. Instead of getting the goods, he is lowballed with a nearly two-minute-long soliloquy that explains exactly nothing. After this blustering exposition that only succeeds at exposing the writer's hand, Cloud, who is very obviously suffering from a mental breakdown, reverses roles to take pity on our intrepid writers by assuring them that, 'You got this.'

From the earliest chapters, Cloud swings his sword at thin air, shouting at 'Whispers' onlookers cannot see, inexplicably refusing to explain himself, and exhibiting severe psychotic breaks in full view of the public despite Sephiroth being miles away. But even when Sephiroth *is* pimped out to the player it's revealed that other characters have absolutely no awareness of the event, not even when Cloud hallucinates a desperate Aerith being cornered by the ghosts of the writer's talent, with a seven-foot-tall leather daddy grabbing hold of her shoulder to stoke Cloud's latent character development. To characters denied the player's meta-knowledge, this 'Mako-junkie' (yes, a citizen literally calls him that) no longer looks like a sane mercenary, much less a veteran commander. He looks like a permanently unhinged, self-generating liability whose behavior should rightfully incur a mountain of lawsuits and property damage claims before he can even make it out of Sector 7.

If that was the baseline for the first game, then the sequel treats it as a dare. Insanely, as if digging an endless series of plot holes could somehow even out this bizarre sequence of hyper-stylized revisions, the writers reduce the original story to the narrative equivalent of the Gold Saucer, except with graffiti scribbled in crayon on top. This structural erosion reaches a point of no return at the Gongaga Reactor in *Rebirth*, where Cloud outright becomes a serial killer, Tifa has a magical whale moment, and the player is left to assume the story was entirely written with AI, except that would be an insult to AI. The writers managed to produce characters so viscerally inhuman that even robots had to learn to blush.

In fact, by the time they are done with Cloud, if they had not been restrained by the company targeting the game towards a younger audience, it's likely that they would have left him drenched in blood after lapping the life leaking from the remains of his victims. They settle instead on him going on a modest killing spree after a hallucination of Sephiroth secretly convinces him nothing is real, ending with a casual stabbing of a defenseless Shinra man in the back as Tifa cries out for him to stop. After this seminal and inescapable turn to murder, and with a cinematic splatter of blood spraying across his face, Tifa, naturally, takes this opportunity to get right up next to him and immediately begins exposing her belly, as victims are prone to do in extreme proximity to a predator. But when it finally dawns on her that he's still hungry for blood, she completely forgets how to defend herself because the game is in a cutscene. Of course, Cloud chases this ample opportunity to continue his newfound fancy for carnage by taking a lethal, full-force swipe that would have cut her in half if the invisible writer's strings hadn't pulled her off the conveniently open-rail platform she had for some reason run to stand on the edge of. The camera cuts on what would have been full contact between the sword and her mid-section, and then perplexingly jumps to her harmlessly falling into a pit of Mako below, her eyes fully dilated from whatever the plot has her smoking off-screen. Once she splashes into the liquid plot armor, she momentarily forgets how to swim before remembering that she has at least a little agency left in her, and then pops back up for a breath of fresh air just to suddenly be swallowed up by a mythological leviathan that gave absolutely zero indication of its presence just moments before. And then, finally, the previously restrained writers allow themselves to embark on a journey of self-discovery.

Tifa's magical whale moment speaks for itself. Not even the most cunning satirical wit could possibly do it justice. It utterly defies reason and suspiciously renders Cloud's story as a chapter in the writer's autobiography, which, like this sequence, they hope will end with zero physical or narrative consequence.

After all is said and done, and Cloud's maniacal bloodlust has been incomprehensibly satiated, *Rebirth* brings the aftermath of this latest in a string of psychotic breaks up to a new level of jaw-dropping negligence. Barret, Aerith, Yuffie, and whatever else can be described as a teammate in this parody, allow this known madman to sit comfortably behind closed doors in Tifa's bed chambers entirely unsupervised, as he thoughtfully stares at her like a lustful lycanthrope peering at a sleeping moon. Suddenly Tifa wakes up and the very first thing she starts to do is babble out a highly specific question that absolutely could somehow maybe be formulated right after awakening from a coma, inquiring if he remembers something or another about their shared past over five years ago. This stellar dialogue is about as passionate and appropriately rendered as her unmoving alien plastic face as she uncannily peers past the ceiling towards her home galaxy. Naturally, Cloud proceeds to have a casual discussion with this big-eyed visitor about something wholly unrelated to the shocking events that led up to her infirmity.

What is glaringly missing is that he had just tried to slaughter her and almost had her killed after an extremely dangerous situation that he caused and which needed to be addressed, but oh no. We need Tifa to explicitly call him a hero from out of nowhere because the writers suddenly realized that maybe they needed to reassure the player about the massacre they just witnessed. So don't worry about the bloodstains, just listen to the extra-terrestrial telling you you're a champ. Finally, Cloud has the sense to broach the subject of the six-foot cleaver he literally brought into the room housing his victim, but what is this? He meekly asks, what kind of hero 'pushes' someone into a pool of Mako? *Pushes?* He utterly fails to grasp the fact that he went on a killing spree and attempted to brutally sever her in half. Then, in a stunning admission, albeit lacking the horrifying musical backtrack it deserves, he describes how he doesn't know who he is, that he forgets things, and then flat out gives it all away by expressly stating, 'it's like there's different people inside of me.'

Instead of responding with immediate shock and horror and running to the team to demand answers about why they let this madman into the room with her, she gets all cute and sits forward in a flirty little posture and asks him to go on, clearly in heat at the idea of this thoroughly broken psychopath opening up to her. It is at this point that we realize they have all flat-out lost their minds, and we are watching the origin story of a band of roving lunatics. It's almost as if they mixed up the script with an upcoming horror release, and now Tifa is the matron mastermind of their new cult. Somehow, someway, and with some absolutely twisted logic, the purpose of the random backstory is now understood, as she uses it as proof that Cloud once saved her, entirely skipping over the fact that he has since tried to murder her, so the writers can use their puppet to declare that it's now her turn to save him. And then they move to romantically seal the deal with the devil by imparting a kiss, except they are interrupted by an eavesdropping Yuffie and Cait Sith, who, now fully privy to this insane conversation, are used merely for comedic effect. It is not understood if the writers intended them to break up some sexy love scene or if they simply needed them to stop themselves from going any further down this path of exploring a completely bonkers, deranged, off the wall, and honestly masterful depiction of their own sheer high-grade psychosis.

After this ultimate rejection of what made these characters so great in the original game, *Rebirth* is no longer worth proceeding with. All of that depth from 1997 was treated as so much trash tossed away, along with the core convictions of the characters. The party never held a group intervention to question Cloud's unraveling mind. Even when defenders of the narrative argue that Tifa’s Lifestream journey was a moment of cosmic understanding that transcended the need for confrontation, they fail to explain how that should have erased the basic survival instincts of the rest of the group. They may rely on shielding Cloud’s volatility under the umbrella of SOLDIER 'cellular degradation,' but the writers still crippled the competence of the cast. Tifa's forgiveness could not explain why seasoned survivalists like Barret and Aerith allowed a degrading Cloud to walk out of that reactor still holding the very weapon he had just tried to kill her with. It could not explain why they allowed him near her unconscious body after she materialized from the Lifestream, much less why they left him alone with her behind closed doors while she lay defenseless in bed, *still* with his weapon. By trading logical group accountability for manufactured melodrama, the writers prioritized immediate cinematic shock over basic psychological reasoning, rendering a legendary rebel faction complicit in their own leader’s unchecked madness. *Rebirth*'s greatest advocates, who once perceived Cloud as a young man struggling with a vulnerability and protected by love, must now see him as an unconvincing depiction of mental illness, enabled by a cast of characters that behave as anything but human.

## Denied Rebirth: From a Eulogy to an Autopsy
A dignified sci-fi tragedy has been transformed into a frustrating spectacle of mental melodrama. In 1997, Cloud Strife was never meant to be an aloof, brooding loner. He was an awkward, introverted, yet deeply reliable guy who people looked up to. He happened to suffer from severe traumatic brain injury. But it was his leadership, his bravery, his athleticism, his love, his loyalty, even his desire for revenge, these are what defined him. Not his illness. But now that a titan of gaming has been brutally assaulted, murdered, and his corpse defiled, we are left with an unpredictable psychopath *solely* defined by his illness. Look at all that we've lost.

This infatuation with mental illness has brought catastrophic damage upon the entire franchise, worse than anything Meteor could ever do. It completely breaks world logic by forcing a hardened rebel vanguard to cede tactical command to a maniac who is known to be actively hallucinating. It completely liquidates the credibility of the cast. Cloud’s unprovoked violence destroys his credibility as a commander, while Aerith’s calculated secrecy destroys her credibility as a teammate. The remake leaves the party entirely stranded between two compromised extremes: a leader who actively tries to execute them, and an oracle who refuses to tell them why. Consequently, companions are reduced to tools only to be used and abused, forced to ignore a screaming, chronic red flag for the sake of the next cinematic set piece. Tifa Lockhart has been stripped of her status as a survivor. Aerith Gainsborough has been robbed of her office as advisor. Critics continue to cheer as each character is extinguished, one after another after another. Who needs a villain like Sephiroth, when this is all we have left?

Ultimately, these remakes reduce a tragic, triumphant celebration of peak human performance into a spectacle of helpless clinical weakness. The original classic functioned as a eulogy, a somber, deeply respectful meditation on human grief, memory loss, and the collective effort to save the world at great personal cost. The modern trilogy is a corporate autopsy. It dissects what was a living, breathing masterpiece into harvested parts for cheap tricks. By leaving behind the profound subtleties of human psychology, the remakes did not revitalize a legend. They murdered a hero, left his corpse a psychopath, and spawned a story that is no longer human. In the wake of Cloud Strife's assassination, his enemies dance on his corpse and on the cadavers of his allies. Shinra finally wins. So it's left to us to remember him for who he and his friends were, or perhaps are, for those of us yet to discover them for the very first time.
